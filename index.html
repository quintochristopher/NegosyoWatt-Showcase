01 Day 1 · Getting Started

Start with the bill,
not a spreadsheet.

Ana registers her laundromat and enters her latest electricity bill. She adds the appliances she relies on: washing machines, a dryer, lights, and fans.

NegosyoWatt uses appliance ratings and usage assumptions to estimate operating costs—not to replace the actual bill.

02 Day 2 · Finding the Drivers

See where the
estimate adds up.

On her appliance list, Ana can compare estimated monthly energy use and cost. The dryer stands out: its 2,200 W rating and four hours of daily use add up to about 264 kWh—or ₱3,783.12 a month at her current rate.

Her appliance estimates total 444 kWh, more than the 200 kWh on her bill. She checks the ratings and usage assumptions before relying on the estimate.

03 Days 3–4 · Testing a Change

Try a change
before committing.

Ana tests a shorter dryer schedule in the simulator. Reducing estimated use from four hours to three and a half projects about ₱472.89 in monthly savings.

She weighs that estimate against drying time and customer waits, then puts the change into practice. She records it as planned—the projected savings remain an estimate until future bills show what changed.

04 Day 5 and Beyond · Keeping Track

Let real bills
tell the story.

Ana keeps entering her bills and compares actual expenses across billing periods. Over time, she can see whether costs changed after her operational adjustment.

Actual bills, appliance estimates, and projected scenario savings stay clearly separated—so the next decision is based on better information.
